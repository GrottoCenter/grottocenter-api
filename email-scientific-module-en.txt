Subject: Grottocenter "Scientific Data" module — alpha version open to contributors

Hello,

The Wikicaves team has been working for several months on a new Grottocenter module dedicated to scientific data from underground environments and their surroundings. We've reached a functional alpha stage and are looking for scientists willing to contribute their data and test the visualization and analysis tool.

What the module supports today:

- Storing time series measurements (temperature, humidity, atmospheric pressure, CO₂, rainfall, water level, conductivity, pH…) linked to geolocated observation points associated with caves.
- Managing associated metadata: measurement device, sensor configuration (unit, precision, detection limits), sampling interval, data quality.
- Recording contamination observations (heavy metals, hydrocarbons, nitrates, microplastics…) and human activities.
- Hosting population monitoring data (bats, cave invertebrates, entrance flora…) as part of biospeleological observations.
- Visualizing and analyzing data through a BI tool (Apache Superset) integrated into Grottocenter — interactive dashboards with time filters, time series charts, and geolocated maps.

What we offer testers:

Each contributor receives a personal account on the BI tool. You're not limited to viewing pre-built dashboards: you can explore your own data, create your own charts, and build your own dashboards, which you can then share on the relevant cave pages in Grottocenter.

What we expect in return:

- Real datasets from cave or surface monitoring stations (data loggers, CO₂ sensors, rain gauges, water level probes…), or population monitoring data (counts, inventories, periodic surveys). Accepted formats: Excel (.xlsx/.xls), CSV.
- Feedback on the data model structure: do the concepts (observation point, time series, sensor configuration) match the way you work?
- Feedback on the analysis tool: which visualizations are useful, which are missing, what data cross-references would you like to perform?

Guiding principles:

- Open data: like all Grottocenter data, scientific data is licensed under CC-BY-SA 3.0. The goal is to pool knowledge about underground environments.
- Interoperability: the model relies on standard ontologies (QUDT for physical quantities, SOSA/SSN for observations, Darwin Core for biospeleology, UIS Speleology Ontology for human activities).
- Durability: data is stored in a partitioned PostgreSQL database, hosted on professional cloud infrastructure with regular automated backups, designed to accommodate millions of measurements over the long term.
- Autonomy: each researcher has their own analysis workspace, without depending on the technical team to produce visualizations.
- Collaboration: multiple users can work on the same dataset or dashboard, enabling cross-analysis and teamwork between researchers.

How to participate:

Reply to this email with:
1. A brief description of your data (type of measurements or surveys, number of points, time period covered, frequency, device or sensor reference).
2. A sample file if possible.
3. The names and email addresses of the people you'd like us to create accounts for.
4. Your questions or comments about the model.

We handle the initial import and create accounts on the analysis tool for the people you indicate, so they can validate the results and start exploring the data.

This module is under active development — your feedback at this stage will directly shape its final form. Now is the ideal time to contribute.
